# Resilient and adversarial comms landscape

A synthesis of a five-stream research sweep (August 2026) into resilient and disruption-tolerant networking, networks-as-code and intent-based networking, adversarial mesh security, LoRa and LPWAN prior art, and adjacent off-grid ecosystems. It exists to point NephMesh at the ideas worth adopting, name what it already covers so it is not re-derived, and be honest about what is impractical at LoRa scale or overclaimed in the current docs.

Sourcing caveat: the sweep's shared web-search budget capped early, so most claims are grounded in direct fetches of primary sources (RFCs, NASA, arXiv, project docs, GitHub Security Advisories, the GitHub API) rather than search-result pages. Load-bearing claims carry links; a few paywalled papers are cited by DOI. Where a fact was not re-verifiable this pass it is flagged.

## The two highest-confidence findings

Two themes recurred across independent streams and the canonical literature, so they carry the most weight.

**1. Airtime is the real wall, and airtime governance is NephMesh's clearest differentiator.** A LoRa mesh does not fail from node count; it fails from airtime and collisions. The founding scaling study (Bor et al., "Do LoRa LPWANs Scale?", MSWiM 2016, [doi:10.1145/2988287.2989157](https://doi.org/10.1145/2988287.2989157)) puts a single cell's ceiling near 120 nodes; a 2026 Meshtastic preset link-budget study ([arXiv:2605.17063](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.17063)) shows the longest-range presets have the longest airtime and therefore collapse a dense channel fastest (range and node count are in direct tension, and failure is a cliff, not a slope); the EU 10% duty cycle is enforced in firmware and Meshtastic only auto-scales broadcast intervals above 40 nodes. The reports that studied routing (resilient-comms) and LoRa scaling (LoRa prior art) both landed on the same recommendation independently: **model the shared channel's airtime like a Kubernetes resource quota and make it an enforced reconciler invariant**, so the control plane refuses or reshapes any fleet change (added nodes, a longer-range preset, extra telemetry) that would oversubscribe the channel. Hand-tuned Meshtastic cannot do this; a declarative intent system is exactly what can.

**2. The multi-driver ("radio-agnostic seam") thesis is validatable now.** Two mature, structurally different LoRa firmwares have documented control surfaces and no existing operator: **MeshCore** (hybrid flood-then-source-routing, up to 63 hops, a get/set CLI plus a binary companion protocol, claimed tens of thousands of users) and, for LoRaWAN, **ChirpStack v4** (a gRPC-first, idempotent object model that maps almost one-to-one onto CRDs, with a genuinely empty automation gap: no operator, Helm chart, or Terraform provider, verified via the GitHub API this pass). Building either as a second driver proves the seam is real rather than Meshtastic-specific.

## Resilience expressed as validated desired-state (the meta-idea)

The single pattern that unifies the resilient-comms findings, and that plays to NephMesh's strength, is: **express resilience mechanisms as policy the control plane can author, simulate, and enforce at near-zero airtime, and borrow the doctrine rather than the bandwidth-hungry machinery.** Concretely, adopt as declarative desired-state:

- **A store-and-forward bundle abstraction with custody and TTL** (Bundle Protocol v7 semantics, [RFC 9171](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/rfc9171/)) rather than a full BPv7 stack on every node. NASA now flies DTN operationally (PACE mission, [NASA DTN](https://www.nasa.gov/communicating-with-missions/delay-disruption-tolerant-networking/)) and mandates it for LunaNet; the right terrestrial move is to adopt the vocabulary and reserve a convergence-layer seam, piloting a microcontroller BPv7 stack ([uD3TN], [DTN7](https://dtn7.github.io/)) only at gateway nodes, never leaf nodes. Reticulum's LXMF is a working, LoRa-proven store-and-forward message layer to use as a reference design rather than rebuilding one.
- **Data mules and scheduled contacts as first-class intent** (a phone or vehicle physically carrying messages between disconnected islands is the highest-bandwidth link in a disaster and costs no airtime; this is DTN Contact Graph Routing applied to predictable assets).
- **Routing roles, hop limits, and region presets as governed, airtime-validated policy.** The number-one real-world Meshtastic failure is too many rebroadcasters and hop limits set too high; encoding "sparse, well-placed routers" as validated policy is pure control-plane leverage with no new radio protocol. Use the 2025 features (NextHop routing, ROUTER_LATE, Zero-Cost Hops) as knobs the controller assigns from a coverage intent.
- **Distinct DDIL degradation policies, not a binary up/down.** Denied, Degraded, Intermittent, and Limited are four different failure modes wanting four different automated responses (shed telemetry vs lean on store-and-forward vs go listen-only). Public tactical doctrine now treats DIL as the baseline condition (REDCOM, [MWI: Reimagining Contested Communications](https://mwi.westpoint.edu/reimagining-contested-communications/)).
- **EMCON ("quiet mode") as a declarative operating state.** A disciplined listen-only or minimal-emission mode serves three payoffs from one primitive: hostile-environment survivability, LoRa duty-cycle compliance, and battery life.
- **Baran's redundancy level (roughly three independent paths per critical node) as a measured resilience KPI**, computed on the intended topology in the sim-first pipeline (graph cut-sets, k-connectivity) so "is the mesh survivable?" becomes a number, not an article of faith. This is the founding survivability result (Baran, On Distributed Communications, RAND 1964) made measurable.
- **BLE-plus-LoRa two-tier offload.** Current research reaches hundreds of nodes only by keeping 80 to 90% of traffic on BLE and reserving LoRa for the backbone ([arXiv:2604.15532](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.15532)); most Meshtastic hardware already has BLE. Reserving scarce LoRa airtime for inter-cluster backhaul directly attacks the airtime wall.

## Security: treat the RF medium as untrusted

Our assume-breach work stops at the cluster boundary (RBAC, secret redaction, hostile-CR rejection, transmit interlock). The adversarial-security stream's central finding is that **the real trust decision happens the moment automation believes a packet off the air**, and that layer is currently untrusted.

- **Meshtastic's crypto is AES-256-CTR with a shared PSK: no per-sender authentication, no integrity MAC (CTR is malleable, so bit-flips go undetected), and no replay protection** (dedup is only a 32-bit packet id plus a 3-bit hop limit). The docs say plainly that anyone with the channel key can impersonate anyone else. Recent CVEs sharpen this: duplicate/low-entropy keypairs (CVE-2025-52464, CVSS 9.5), unauthenticated public-key overwrite via crafted NodeInfo (CVE-2025-55293), a PKI-to-legacy silent downgrade (CVE-2025-53627), and forced HAM/unencrypted mode (CVE-2025-55292). Sources: [Meshtastic encryption](https://meshtastic.org/docs/overview/encryption/), [firmware security advisories](https://github.com/meshtastic/firmware/security/advisories).
- **Highest-value control: a NephMesh application-layer authentication and freshness envelope on every mesh packet that can trigger automation** (a per-node signature plus a monotonic counter or timestamp; reject anything unsigned, stale, or replayed at ingest). It neutralizes the impersonation, replay, and PKI-downgrade cluster in one testable move (forge or replay a command, assert it is rejected), and it holds even on unpatched or vendor-cloned firmware.
- **Epoch-keyed channels for forward secrecy and revocation at zero airtime.** Derive each epoch's key from a root on a time boundary and on every membership change, and delete the parent (the Briar Bramble Transport Protocol model: no online rekey traffic). Rotating a flat PSK still lets a captured key decrypt everything; epoch derivation bounds exposure to one window, and rekey-on-membership-change is the only real revocation on a shared-key broadcast medium. Full MLS or Double-Ratchet post-compromise security is not achievable on a Meshtastic channel without forking firmware, so epoch KDF is the pragmatic answer.
- **The receive-only SDR as an out-of-band trust anchor.** Because the SDR is not a mesh member, a compromised insider holding the shared PSK cannot lie to it. It can reconcile what nodes claim against what was actually transmitted (a "claim vs air" IDS): duplicate node id (one id, two distinct RF signatures), impossible mobility, and Sybil (many ids, one radio fingerprint). This reframes the co-located SDR from a spectrum instrument into a trust anchor the mesh cannot spoof, and the transmit interlock keeps it entirely passive. A corollary myth-bust from the SDR stream: a HackRF (even an RTL-SDR) can actually **decode** a single LoRa channel, not just sense energy ([gr-lora_sdr](https://github.com/tapparelj/gr-lora_sdr), [SDRangel ChirpChat](https://github.com/f4exb/sdrangel/blob/master/plugins/channelrx/demodchirpchat/readme.md)), so the sensor can produce decode-verified packet ground truth, not only occupancy.
- **MQTT egress minimization.** The bridge uplinks every raw packet it hears; a private broker enforces none of the public server's privacy filters, so it is the largest one-config exfiltration hole. Default-deny position and telemetry uplink, require encryption, and allowlist egress.

## The closed loop: anti-oscillation and anti-herding

Our threat model already names the jam-to-herd attack and requires hysteresis, dwell, rate-limiting, and Porch approval. Two streams add the concrete stability mechanism and one uncomfortable truth: a well-formed, legal, adversarially-induced intent passes admission (the threat is upstream of admission), and prompt reconciliation will faithfully execute an induced move unless explicitly slowed.

- **An intent debouncer in front of the reconciler: dual-threshold hysteresis (a Schmitt-trigger deadband), a dwell timer (3GPP time-to-trigger style), and a cool-down.** This is the one component that decides whether a spectrum-to-intent loop is stable or a noise-driven thrash machine, and it protects the human-approval queue from fatigue. Grounded in decades of control theory (anti-ping-pong handover design) and BGP Route Flap Damping ([RFC 2439](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc2439)), with the essential counter-lesson of [RIPE-378](https://www.ripe.net/publications/docs/ripe-378/): an over-aggressive damper became worse than the disease, so a circuit-breaker that freezes on last-good config and alerts is safer than an ever-tightening one.
- **Unpredictable, non-adversary-controllable destination selection.** An attacker may force a move but must not control which move; pick the target channel from a secret or rotating order and blocklist any channel the attacker just "cleared." Damping denies a fast repeatable trigger; unpredictable target denies control of where you land.
- **A jam-versus-congestion classifier** using multi-metric consistency checks (Xu et al., MobiHoc 2005): low delivery with high RSSI and rising CRC errors reads as jamming, low delivery with low RSSI as fading, low delivery with normal RSSI and backoff as congestion. Most herding relies on making benign pressure look like a reason to move; the classifier denies the cheap trigger. This is the cognitive-radio Primary User Emulation attack, and the defenses (hard-to-forge corroboration, transmit-power and location fingerprints) apply.

## Prior art and lineage: the Reticulum reckoning

The completeness stream's sharpest finding is that **Reticulum is our biggest blind spot and a knowledgeable reviewer will ask "why not Reticulum?" on first read.** Reticulum ([reticulum.network](https://reticulum.network/)) already ships much of our stated north star: self-sovereign cryptographic addressing (a hash of a public key, no central authority), encryption by default, a medium-agnostic transport that spans LoRa (RNode firmware) to TCP/IP transparently, next-hop-only self-healing routing, source-address-free packets, and usability down to a few bits per second. Its self-sovereign addressing is also the real answer to the shared-key impersonation weakness our threat model admits.

The honest position NephMesh must state in writing: **Reticulum is the closest philosophical neighbor, and it is a candidate managed driver, not a competitor.** RNodes run on the same hardware as Meshtastic and have a first-class remote-management plane (an identity-hash allow-list, `rnx`/`rnsh`, a Python API) that maps cleanly onto a reconcile loop, so "an RNode as another CRD" is coherent. What NephMesh adds on top of any such transport is the Kubernetes and GitOps management plane and the SDR feedback loop. One caveat before any security claim built on it: Reticulum's links use AES-256-CBC plus HMAC, not a modern AEAD, and its group destinations use a shared symmetric key (the same tradeoff Meshtastic has).

Other lineage worth citing rather than re-deriving: **Baran** (distributed survivability, redundancy level), **DTN/BPv7** (store-carry-forward at planetary delay), **Briar/Bramble** (offline-first, out-of-band contact verification), and **Serval Rhizome** (epidemic store-and-forward, a reference for the application-level inbox). Two cautionary dead ends: **disaster.radio** (repo seeking a maintainer, domain not resolving) and **Commotion** (wound down), useful as honest evidence of how community LoRa/mesh projects lose momentum, and a reason NephMesh manages a thriving existing firmware rather than building new hardware. **goTenna** is now defense-only (acquired, consumer mesh discontinued).

## Intent-based networking and networks-as-code alignment

- **Enforce the intent-versus-policy boundary** ([RFC 9315](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc9315/)): intent declares the desired outcome and excludes how; the CRDs should declare comms outcomes (coverage, channel, reachability, spectrum constraints), never imperative radio commands, or it is a config API wearing an intent label. RFC 9315's inner autonomic loop (drift detection and counteraction) plus outer human loop (Porch approval) is exactly NephMesh's architecture; naming it that way gives a standards-backed story. A validated comms-intent schema or ontology (TMF921-shaped) that rejects malformed intent before Porch is the highest-ROI, lowest-effort standards move.
- **A fleet and RF source-of-truth** (NetBox-style or CRD-backed) holding node inventory, geo, antenna, frequency plan, and the neighbor/link graph. GitOps already stores config; this stores the asset-and-topology data that config assumes but rarely holds, and everything else (verification, twins, coverage intent) needs it.
- **A Batfish-analog pre-merge mesh verifier as a Porch KRM validator**: does the new channel plan keep the mesh connected, respect regional and legal constraints (duty cycle, EIRP, band), and avoid self-interference? This turns human approvals from eyeballing into proofs, and folds the airtime-budget invariant in as a gate. A containerlab-style mesh digital twin in CI would simulate an intent change's routing and coverage impact before it touches hardware.
- **PackageVariantSet with selectors and CEL injection** (Nephio R6, Porch v1.5.6) for fleet fan-out, using the `Ready`/`Stalled` conditions as the human-review queue; and cooperative specializers (condkptsdk) for conflict-free channel and frequency assignment.

## Interop and real users

- **ATAK via Cursor-on-Target, correctly scoped.** ATAK-CIV is the largest field-proven civilian and first-responder situational-awareness ecosystem, credited with thousands of hurricane rescues. The scoping that must not be gotten wrong: Meshtastic carries a compressed `TAKPacket` protobuf on a dedicated portnum over the air, not raw CoT XML (which is far too large for a ~200-byte frame); CoT interop happens off the mesh at the MQTT/gateway edge. Any "we speak CoT" claim must say "compressed TAKPacket on the mesh, CoT at the gateway."
- **CAP (Common Alerting Protocol / ITU-T X.1303)** for emergencies: ingesting official IPAWS/CAP feeds and fanning them over the mesh offline (and optionally emitting CAP) is concrete value to emergency managers, well beyond using CAP's code taxonomy for agent responses.
- **MQTT Sparkplug** birth/death state model fits an intermittently-connected fleet's liveness on NephMesh's own telemetry plane (not Meshtastic's fixed topics).

## Observability: landed enough to measure; the gap moved

The completeness stream originally picked **the mesh-observability layer** as the single most important gap. That is no longer the top gap. The operator publishes readiness, apply, and airtime gauges; `demo/resilience` measures message delivery ratio and reduces it to a verdict; last-heard is a printer column. What remains of that stream is MeshMonitor integration (a cousin dashboard, not a replacement) and richer hop/SNR series.

The ranking that matches the code now: (1) an *enforced* airtime `ChannelBudget` (report-only floor exists; refusal does not); (2) two-cluster control-plane independence (UDP-sim demonstration exists; two clusters do not); (3) a stranger-runnable three-witness RF script (the 2026-08-09 bench ran by hand).

## Day-2 fleet lifecycle

Named "thin" in our own roadmap and confirmed as the gap between a demo and something an EMCOMM or SAR team trusts: firmware rollout, coordinated key/channel/PSK rotation (load-bearing for the security story, which assumes non-default PSKs but never rotates them), node decommissioning, and a PKC admin-key lifecycle driver (provision and rotate the three admin keys per node, handle the reflash-erases-keys gotcha, detect drift). Meshtastic's PKC remote admin (firmware 2.5+) is the actuation verb for this.

## Honest limits and myth-busts (carry these into the docs)

- **Real per-hop LoRa range is single-digit kilometres** (2.47 km measured in a 2026 campus deployment, [arXiv:2605.20379](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20379)), not the marketed tens or hundreds; "hundreds of nodes" is always an off-LoRa-offload number.
- **LoRa's chirp spread spectrum gives processing gain against noise, not anti-jam security.** The chirp is standardized and un-keyed; against an adversary who reads SF/BW by SDR in seconds it provides zero jam resistance, and selective/reactive jamming is cheap (Aras et al., [arXiv:1712.02141](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.02141)). True FHSS is not implemented and FCC 15.247 FHSS rules make it impractical on the stack. The only genuine PHY anti-jam levers on commodity gear are slow slot agility and directional antennas (with the mandatory EIRP reduction above 6 dBi). Do not sell a "jam-proof mesh."
- **Ham-band encryption is prohibited (FCC Part 97); ISM AES is fine (Part 15).** A payload legally encrypted on 915 MHz ISM would be illegal encrypted over Winlink/VARA/JS8Call/APRS. This constrains any ham-radio egress driver to unencrypted content.
- **The satellite market has shaken out.** Buildable now: a Starlink IP dish as backhaul and a live NB-IoT-NTN service (Skylo); the field is consolidating on 3GPP NTN, so an AT-command NB-IoT-NTN modem is the most future-proof driver target. Do not design around Swarm (shut down March 2025) or AST SpaceMobile (pre-commercial). Meshtastic has no native satellite feature; the only seam is its MQTT bridge over a bring-your-own IP pipe.
- **Metadata, direction-finding, and traffic analysis are inherent** to any unlicensed sub-GHz mesh, and Meshtastic has no forward secrecy today, so harvest-now-decrypt-later applies to any captured PSK. RF fingerprinting on commodity SDR is a probabilistic corroborator, never a hard authenticator.
- **Meshtastic ceilings:** 7 hops max (3 recommended; deep chains fail silently), 8 channels sharing the primary's frequency slot (secondary channels are not frequency isolation), JSON-over-MQTT unavailable on nRF52.

## Ranked action backlog (research-informed)

Highest leverage first. Near-term items are hardware-free and fit the current phase; later items are tracked but deferred.

1. **Airtime/duty-cycle budget as an enforced reconciler invariant** (near-term). The one idea backed by two independent streams and the canonical literature; turns the biggest physical constraint into the clearest differentiator. Report-only floor shipped; refusal (a Porch/KRM validator) is the remaining distinctive step.
2. **Two-cluster control-plane independence, then MeshMonitor as a cousin UI** (near-term). The UDP-sim harness and operator metrics landed. The load-bearing remainder is two clusters, MDR unchanged. Do not rebuild MeshMonitor; integrate it if a live dashboard is needed.
3. **Application-layer authentication and freshness envelope on automation-triggering mesh packets** (near-term). Neutralizes the impersonation, replay, and PKI-downgrade cluster; the honest completion of assume-breach.
4. **SDR "claim vs air" ground-truth monitor** (near-term, sim-first), starting with duplicate-node-id and impossible-mobility detection, plus adding a receive-only LoRa decoder alongside the energy sweep. Strengthens the project's actual novelty.
5. **Anti-oscillation debouncer plus unpredictable-destination selection plus jam-versus-congestion classifier for Phase 6** (design near-term). Makes the closed loop stable and un-steerable.
6. **A written Reticulum position and a prior-art update covering the crypto-mesh lineage** (near-term, doc-only). Cheapest credibility fix; closes the obvious reviewer objection.
7. **Epoch-keyed channels as an auditable GitOps rotation intent** (design near-term). Forward secrecy and real revocation on a shared-key medium at zero airtime.
8. **A second radio driver: MeshCore first (same hardware, different routing), or ChirpStack scoped to Gateway-Mesh relay-role intent** (mid-term). Proves the radio-agnostic seam is real.
9. **Day-2 fleet lifecycle: PKC admin-key rotation, firmware rollout, decommissioning** (design near-term, implement later).
10. **ATAK/TAKPacket and CAP interop** (mid-term). The biggest real-user unlocks, correctly scoped.
11. **A validated comms-intent schema and a Batfish-analog pre-merge mesh verifier** (mid-term). Makes it a true intent API and turns approvals into proofs.
12. **A reserved BPv7 convergence-layer seam and a data-mule/scheduled-contact intent** (later). Turns the DTN north star from rhetoric into an extension point.
